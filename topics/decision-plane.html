Decision Plane

The decision plane converts typed institutional context into a proposed choice, assessment, or plan. Sovereign Source AI supports deterministic rules, bounded models, statistical inference, language-model reasoning, and human judgment within this responsibility. The appropriate decider depends on the question, available evidence, permitted data handling, and consequences. Every decision remains subject to external authorization before it becomes an executable action.

Different forms of decision

Deterministic rules are appropriate when the applicable conditions and result are explicit. They provide a direct relationship between recorded inputs, policy, and output. Their usefulness depends on whether the encoded rule accurately represents the intended requirement.

Bounded models answer narrow questions within a defined decision space. They can select among options, score alternatives, or assess a proposition using typed inputs and outputs. The boundary makes their role easier to evaluate and keeps a narrow judgment from expanding into general authority.

Statistical inference estimates relationships, probabilities, or uncertain attributes from observations. Its output remains an inference with supporting evidence and uncertainty. It should remain distinguishable from directly observed or user-declared facts.

Language models can interpret documents, synthesize context, propose plans, and reason about ambiguous situations. Their flexibility does not remove the need for typed outputs, evaluation, or constrained access.

Human judgment addresses ambiguity, institutional tradeoffs, exceptions, and consequential choices. Humans also operate within defined authority. A person making a recommendation and a person approving an action may hold different roles.

Choosing the right decider

Selection begins with the question being asked. An explicit control test may be suitable for deterministic evaluation. A narrow scoring task may fit a bounded model. An uncertain estimate may require statistical analysis. A complex interpretation may benefit from language-model reasoning and human review.

Routing also considers classification, jurisdiction, task, cost, latency, confidence, and risk. A provider must be permitted to receive the relevant information before its reasoning quality becomes useful.

The architecture supports combinations of deciders. Rules can establish eligibility before a model ranks options. A model can explain a deterministic finding. A human can review conflicting assessments. Each contribution should retain its identity and evidence so that the final decision can be reconstructed.

The decision contract

A typed decision records the selected choice or assessment, its provider, the model where applicable, the relevant policy version, and supporting evidence. Probability or confidence can be recorded when meaningful.

These fields make decisions comparable and auditable. They also preserve a distinction between a result and the grounds for accepting it. Reported confidence is one input to evaluation and escalation; it is not authorization.

The reusable path is:

Intent → Typed State → Decision Provider → Policy Gate → Capability → Execute → Verify → Evidence

The provider receives governed state and returns a bounded result. The policy gate determines whether the proposed action is permitted. The capability layer limits what execution can actually do.

Escalation and unresolved decisions

Low confidence can route a question to additional reasoning or human review. High consequence can require human approval even when a provider reports strong confidence.

Escalation should preserve the original question, evidence, uncertainty, and prior assessments. Otherwise, the next reviewer receives a conclusion without the context needed to judge it.

When evidence is insufficient, the system should preserve that limitation. The source research uses explicit unknown results in control assessment, illustrating how an unresolved question can remain visible without becoming an unsupported positive finding.

Reference implementation

A reference build contains typed decision requests, provider adapters, policy-aware routing, evaluation records, and a human review surface. Shared contracts allow rules, models, and humans to participate without embedding one provider into institutional business logic.

Verification checks the quality of the result separately from the authorization of the action. Evidence links both judgments to the eventual outcome.
