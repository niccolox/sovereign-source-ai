Reference Stack and Exit

A reference stack shows how Sovereign Source AI’s responsibilities fit together without making a particular product indispensable. Its purpose is to demonstrate governed execution and retained institutional control. The decisive measure is whether the organization can preserve its intent, knowledge, authority, and evidence while replacing providers, moving runtimes, and continuing critical operations.

Reference implementation

The seven layers map to components with distinct responsibilities:

LayerReference components and roles
IntentTyped intent registry, requirements, experience contracts, and an SSIL interpreter or compiler
GovernanceInstitutional identity, authority records, external policy gates, classifications, and approvals
DecisionRouting among deterministic rules, statistical models, bounded models, reasoning models, and humans
CapabilitySkill registry, declared operation contracts, permission checks, and authenticated tool access
ExecutionDurable workflows, work orders, isolated workers, services, and deployment definitions
VerificationTests, evaluations, security analysis, requirement checks, release gates, and outcome measurement
EvidenceProvenance relationships, component inventories, artifacts, attestations, audit records, and historical outcomes

The ontology connects these components through shared institutional objects. Interfaces present those objects and their authorized actions. Storage preserves current state and historical evidence. Neither the interface nor a model provider becomes the sole repository of institutional logic.

The four durable planes span this stack. Intention establishes purpose, governance constrains action, verification examines results, and evidence preserves what can be reconstructed. Together they support the loop from intent through decision, constraint, execution, verification, and proof.

Seven sovereignty tests

The manifesto’s seven questions become concrete tests of the reference architecture:

  1. Can the primary vendor be replaced without rebuilding the institution? Contracts, ontology definitions, policies, workflows, and evidence remain usable outside that vendor’s application.
  2. Can the runtime move to another environment? Execution definitions, dependencies, state, and policy boundaries remain meaningful at the destination.
  3. Can the organization retain and reconstruct its data and knowledge? Exported records preserve relationships, history, source material, and derivation context.
  4. Can agent permissions and actions be inspected? Work orders identify authorized capabilities, applicable policy, approvals, and recorded actions.
  5. Can models be swapped without rewriting business logic? Typed contracts and routing separate institutional rules from provider-specific behavior.
  6. Can critical functions operate privately or in restricted networks? Required models, dependencies, authorization, and evidence services remain available within the permitted boundary.
  7. Does the organization retain identity, keys, policy, authorization, provenance, and audit? Control of these functions survives a provider relationship ending.

A documented export option is only part of the answer. Credible exit also requires evidence that the exported material can support continued operation.

Replacement paths

Replacement works when institutional meaning remains stable across adapters. A model adapter can change while decision contracts remain. A workflow engine can change while work orders and authority remain. An analytics service can change while experience definitions and historical evidence remain.

Exit also depends on permissions and practical access. Source code without operational state, usable dependencies, keys, or deployment definitions leaves gaps. The relevant unit of portability is the governed system and its retained knowledge.

Product layers

The product structure groups these responsibilities into a Sovereign Core, an Agent and Capability Control Plane, a Software Factory, a Data and Evidence Platform, an Enterprise Bridge, and vertical applications.

The core holds ontology, authority, intent, policy, and evidence. The control plane governs models and capabilities. The factory produces verified artifacts. The data platform preserves state and history. Enterprise bridges connect existing systems, while vertical products specialize the shared architecture for particular domains.

Commercial services can supply any of these components. Their architectural obligation is to preserve inspectability, portable contracts, and a credible replacement path.
